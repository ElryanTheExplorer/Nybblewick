# Premise Options

Three premises, sketched in depth so the school storylines can be tested against each one (**Q-21**, see [[Decision Gates]]). Names come from the [[Canon Sheet]]. Every school storyline note has an "In each premise" section that uses these.

## P1 The Hidden Machine
- **Situation:** the Hollow and the Ring run on old folk magic: lamp-lighting, hedge charms, barrow rites. Nobody asks why a hand shape works. It works because the land is, underneath, a vast patient machine, and the chords are its instructions.
- **Central mystery:** the land is glitching. Stars drift out of their charts, lodestones point wrong, the dead answer with garbled words. Something in the machine is failing, and the orders each see only their own symptom.
- **Nybblewick:** the town grew around the **Wick**, an ancient lamp whose flame answers chords. It burns dim when the game begins and reaches full strength only at the end. In P1 the Wick is a terminal: the one place where the machine listens most clearly.
- **Why chords:** a chord is a word in the land's own tongue. Apprentices count on their fingers in nursery rhymes ("closed-closed-open-open, three") without knowing it is binary ([[Binary to Hex Teaching]]).
- **Hex and seals:** the tally in the grimoire margins turns out to be hex ([[Board Label Lore]]). Seals are circuits ([[PCB Seals]], [[Logic Gate Runes]]); spirits speak in serial frames ([[Serial Spirits]]). The plot is the reveal.
- **The six orders:** each guards one subsystem without knowing it. Astral = the clock and display (the sky), Alchemy = analog chemistry, Bloom = growth rules, Necro = memory and storage, Smith = power, Wayfarer = routing and addressing.
- **Tone:** earnest folk magic first, wonder second, the machine third. The machine is never cold or "fake": it is as alive as a forest.
- **Risks:** the "it was a simulation" cliché; a reveal that makes the folk magic feel silly in hindsight; players who learned hex early "solve" the mystery in hour one.
- **Opening scene:** night of the first frost. Your mentor holds your hand around the Wick's iron collar and folds your fingers: *closed, open, closed, open*. The Wick has burned low for as long as anyone remembers. The dim flame leans toward you. On the third try it steadies, and for one frame shows a row of tiny marks before it settles. Your mentor did not see them. You did.

## P2 The Long Dusk
- **Situation:** a generation ago the **Dimming** ended the age of great spells. The long incantations failed mid-word; towers fell; the Ring's regions went quiet. Only small, practical magic still works.
- **Central problem:** the land is fading, slowly and not cruelly. Roads crumble, springs cool, the dead forget. Can small spells, done well and together, rebuild a world?
- **Nybblewick:** the last lit town in the Hollow. Its lamps are tended every night; each lamplighter's cast is a promise to the dark.
- **Why chords:** the chord is the **remnant tongue**, the shorthand that survived when long incantations broke. Short words still work. Apprentices learn them because there is nothing else.
- **Hex and seals:** old seals are ruins; their marks are a lost script that you relearn letter by letter (rows A-F are "the six lost letters"). Re-reading a seal is archaeology.
- **The six orders:** scattered remnants, each holding one region alone and each half-convinced the others caused the Dimming. Restoration needs them to trust each other again.
- **Tone:** melancholic but hopeful. Every relit lamp stays lit ([[Design Pillars]], "The land answers").
- **Risks:** grief fatigue; a grey palette fighting the pixel art; restoration that feels like obligation instead of joy.
- **Opening scene:** dusk, which in Nybblewick is most of the day. An old lamplighter can no longer close her ring finger. She hands you her pole and teaches you the three lamps she can't light anymore. You light two. The third stays dark, and a child on the step asks if it's broken forever.

## P3 The Wick Fair
- **Situation:** at the end of each contest season the six orders gather in Nybblewick for the **Wick Fair**: one real-world Fair week shared by all players, with contests, markets of gifts and apprenticeships offered at the Wick. In-game seasons stay personal; only contest seasons are shared ([[Canon Sheet]]).
- **Central problem:** gentle stakes. This year the Wick, dim since the prologue, gutters lower at every Fair, the orders are squabbling over who hosts, and the Ring's small troubles (a sulky river spirit, an overgrown orchard) keep pulling you away.
- **Nybblewick:** a festival town that fills to bursting for each Fair week.
- **Why chords:** chords are a craft, like knitting or bell-ringing. Apprentices learn them to join an order and to compete at the Fair.
- **Hex and seals:** fair games (counting rhymes, ring-toss on lettered boards, seal-puzzle stalls). Hex is a craftsman's notation.
- **The six orders:** friendly rival guilds with stalls, banners and grudges going back to a famous pie contest.
- **Tone:** cozy, whimsical, seasonal.
- **Risks:** low stakes thin out Necro and Rot; Detroit-style branching carries less weight; crowded genre.
- **Opening scene:** Fair morning. Six banners, six stalls, one guttering Wick, dimmer than last Fair. A Starwatch astronomer and a Retort brewer are arguing about whose fault it is. Both turn to you, the new apprentice, and ask you to "just try it".

## Criteria for choosing
| Criterion | P1 | P2 | P3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Justifies chords and hex in-fiction | strong: the reveal | medium: remnant tongue | weak: a craft |
| School conflicts with real stakes | strong | strong | weak |
| Fits utility-first, no combat ([[Utility-First Pivot]]) | strong | strong | strong |
| Necro not a villain | strong (memory subsystem) | medium (risk of blame) | medium |
| Supports [[Rune Puzzles]] (D-22) | strong | medium | weak |
| Fits co-op and seasons ([[Seasonal Contest Loop]]) | medium | medium | strong |
| Art cost and mood | medium | risky (grey) | easy |

## Recommendation
**Recommendation:** P1 The Hidden Machine. It is the only premise where the input, the teaching (D-24) and the embedded puzzles (D-22) *are* the plot. Borrow from the others: P2's restoration gives the visible arc ([[Restoration Loop]]); P3's Wick Fair becomes the real-world Fair week at the end of each contest season, home of the [[Seasonal Contest Loop]].

**Q-40:** In P1, when does the machine reveal land? Options: (a) gradual, hinted from the opening, named in the last act; (b) a mid-game midpoint reveal, then the second half plays with open hex; (c) never named, only shown. Lean (b): it matches the [[Prompt Ladder]] fading to hex around the same point, so teaching and story peak together (ties Q-18).
